---
title: Columns
description: Drag columns by their headers or by the rows of the column panel — including hidden columns.
---

Columns drag on two surfaces over one `columnOrder`: the **header** and the **column panel**. Both
assume the adapter is bound as in [Installation](/docs/data-grid/drag-and-drop). Each option is in the
[API reference](/docs/data-grid/drag-and-drop/api).

## The short form

Turn the column axis on and every header gets a grip before its label. Add
`ordering.column.visibilityMenu` and the Columns toggle in the toolbar becomes a panel whose rows drag
too — no children, no extra components:

<DataGridDocsExample exampleId='column-panel-drag-basic' />

```tsx
<DataGrid
	features={features}
	data={data}
	columns={columns}
	visibility
	ordering={{ column: { visibilityMenu: true } }}
/>
```

With a grip on the header, the column menu drops **Move left** / **Move right** — they are the same
move one step at a time, and the drag is keyboard-operable too. The panel does the same with its
arrows. Each surface takes two overrides:

| You want                     | Header                               | Panel                                               |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------- |
| The grip and the move pair   | `ordering.column.moveControls: true` | `ordering.column.visibilityMenu.moveControls: true` |
| The move pair alone, no drag | `ordering.column.drag: false`        | `ordering.column.visibilityMenu.drag: false`        |

## Hidden columns

The panel lists hidden columns too, so a hidden column can be dragged there and nowhere else.
`Joined` starts hidden; move it in the panel and the visible order rearranges around it. `Id` is
locked with `ordering: false` and offers no grip on either surface:

<DataGridDocsExample exampleId='column-panel-drag' />

A kit rendering its own panel composes each row from `<DataGrid.VisibilityItem>`. Render one item per
listed column, all of them: filtering the list — a search box over a long panel — stops panel
dragging with no error. Filter what a row renders, not which rows exist.

## A header you compose

Compose `<DataGrid.HeaderCell>` and the grip is yours to place: its render function hands back
`dragHandle`, or place your kit's `<ColumnDragHandle />`. Not inside `column.header` — that content
renders within the sort trigger's `<button>`, and a button inside a button is invalid HTML.

<DataGridDocsExample exampleId='column-drag' />

```tsx
<DataGrid.HeaderCell header={header}>
	{({ dragHandle, sortTrigger, menu }) => (
		<DataGrid.HeaderMain>
			{dragHandle}
			{sortTrigger}
			{menu}
		</DataGrid.HeaderMain>
	)}
</DataGrid.HeaderCell>
```

In the example, the leaves under `Details` move among themselves but cannot be dropped under
`Money`, and the resizer still resizes: a drag starts from the handle and nothing else.

## Right-to-left

<DataGridDocsExample exampleId='column-drag-rtl' />

The column axis is logical, so under `dir="rtl"` the later a column sits in the order, the further
left it is drawn — and dragging leftwards moves a column later. Pass `direction='rtl'` to the grid
beside the `dir` on an ancestor, the same pair [column pinning](/docs/data-grid/pinning/columns) needs.

## What a column drag will not do

- **Cross a pin band.** A move from the centre into a pinned edge would read as a pin, not a reorder.
- **Leave a header group.** A leaf moves among its siblings.
- **Move a locked column.** A system column or one declared `ordering: false` renders no handle.

**An illegal drop does not snap back.** The column travels as far as its group or band allows and
commits there. Release outside the grid, or press `Escape`, and nothing is written.
